The Shelf and the Driveway

The toy aisle is stuck in 1969. Australian driveways are not.

Ze Xiang Li · 35042176 · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · September 2026

A toy catalogue records which cars a culture romanticised. The road records which ones it actually bought. This page puts the two side by side on a shared decade axis: 676 Hot Wheels castings against 22.3 million vehicles registered in Australia in January 2025. They turn out to have almost nothing in common. Written for a general Australian audience — no knowledge of die-cast collecting assumed.

Sources and method

Source A — Hot Wheels Casting Database

Orange Track Diecast (orangetrackdiecast.com). Fields used: casting, debut year, designer, real-car decade. 676 rows. Transparency note: this source is published as HTML tables on the web page, not as a downloadable file. It was transcribed to CSV rather than downloaded.

Source B — Road Vehicles, Australia, January 2025

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics, via data.gov.au. Licensed CC BY 3.0 Australia. Fields used: state/territory, registered postcode, make, model, motive power, year of manufacture, vehicle count. Analysis is restricted to passenger vehicles throughout.

Boundaries

Postcode (POA) 2021 boundaries from the Australian Bureau of Statistics, simplified and converted to TopoJSON with mapshaper. All three maps use a conic equal-area projection with standard parallels at 18°S and 36°S.

Derived fields and judgement calls

  • Brand origin (chart 7) is assigned by hand for the top 15 makes. It is not a field in the BITRE data. Every other field on this page comes directly from a source.
  • Collaborations (charts 2 and 3) are credited to the first-named designer. 11 of 676 castings list two designers.
  • Averages are vehicle-weighted throughout — means of means would over-weight small rural postcodes, which are exactly the ones with the oldest fleets.
  • Bin map cells (chart 6) are equal in degrees, not equal in area. The measure is a share, so no area standardising is required.

Limitations

  • The census date is 31 January 2025, so vehicles built late in 2024 were not yet registered. The stream graph's narrowing at its right edge is a collection artifact, not a market contraction. 2025 build years are excluded entirely.
  • 11 non-residential postcodes (PO box and mail-centre codes, 0.06% of vehicles) have no ABS boundary and are excluded from the maps.
  • Bin map cells holding fewer than 500 vehicles are suppressed, which leaves gaps in remote Australia rather than showing noisy percentages.
  • Source A is a sample of the Hot Wheels catalogue, not the complete catalogue. Shelf shares describe this sample.
